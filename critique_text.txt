Technical and Scholarly Audit
Mechanized Formalization of Asymmetric Cross-Energy Operators, Dirichlet Dualities, and Universal Off-Line Zero Locators for the Riemann Zeta Function in Lean 4
Verdict: Reject in present form
A source-based critique of the paper, Lean declarations, repository structure, reproducibility claims, and publication presentation
Prepared 2 August 2026
Executive verdict
Recommendation: Reject as a research paper. Accept only after a complete reframing as a small Lean tutorial or exploratory code note, unless the authors add substantial new mathematics and formalize it honestly.
What is sound and worth preserving
The repository contains readable Lean code organized into small modules.
The conjugation lemma for finite Dirichlet polynomials is the most technically substantive original declaration in the project.
Several statements are valid elementary consequences of complex conjugation, norm multiplicativity, and the completed-zeta functional equation.
The paper does acknowledge that its finite algebraic identities do not prove the Guth-Maynard zero-density estimate.
The source files visible in the repository do not contain an explicit sorry token.
Why that is nowhere near enough
Correctness of a weak theorem does not make an inflated interpretation correct.
A machine-checked tautology remains a tautology. Formal verification certifies the stated proposition, not the paper’s rhetoric about what the proposition means.
The paper does not establish a bridge from its finite Dirichlet polynomials to the Riemann zeta function, large-value estimates, zero-density counting, or prime distribution.
The repository provides no active CI evidence, no axiom audit, no build transcript, no exact paper-to-commit link, and almost no documentation.
The manuscript omits the reference list needed to support its historical, priority, and novelty claims.
Review basis and limits
This audit reviewed the six Lean modules, package entrypoint, Lake configuration, manifest, toolchain file, diagnostic script, nested workflow, Markdown manuscript, and the supplied Word manuscript. It compared those materials against official Mathlib documentation for the Riemann zeta function and nonvanishing theorem, official Lean documentation for axiom and sorry checking, official GitHub documentation for workflow discovery, the lean-action documentation, and the Guth-Maynard publication record. Source keys appear in the final section.
I did not independently execute lake build. The build container available for this audit could not resolve GitHub, so the project’s compile status is treated as unverified rather than false. This limitation strengthens, rather than weakens, the demand for functioning public CI and committed verification evidence.

Detailed findings
1. The paper is about finite Dirichlet polynomials, not the Riemann zeta function  [Critical]
Evidence: The cross-energy, conjugation, large-value, and zero-product declarations are parameterized by an arbitrary coefficient function a and a finite set S. No theorem instantiates a with zeta coefficients, proves convergence, or relates the finite sum to riemannZeta. [R-AE; R-DD]
Why it matters: The title and conclusion attach the prestige and difficulty of the Riemann zeta function to results that do not analyze zeta.
Repair: Remove zeta from the main title, or add a mathematically meaningful specialization and approximation theorem connecting the finite objects to zeta.
2. “Operator” is the wrong mathematical category  [Critical]
Evidence: crossEnergy returns a real number. It is not a map acting on a vector, function, or space in the operator-theoretic sense. No linearity, boundedness, adjoint, spectrum, kernel, or composition is defined. [R-AE 12-14]
Why it matters: Calling a scalar-valued expression an operator creates a false impression of functional analysis or operator theory.
Repair: Rename it crossNormProduct or define a genuine operator with a stated domain and codomain.
3. “Energy” is unmotivated and internally inconsistent  [Major]
Evidence: dirichletEnergy is a squared norm, while crossEnergy is a product of unsquared norms. The manuscript supplies no physical, probabilistic, or analytic convention explaining why both are energies. [R-DD 19-21; R-AE 12-14]
Why it matters: The terminology is decorative rather than explanatory.
Repair: Choose one precise definition, justify it, and use names such as normSquare and crossNormProduct unless an actual energy interpretation is established.
4. There is no cross interaction  [Major]
Evidence: The so-called cross-energy is completely factorized. It contains no integral, inner product, sum over cross terms, covariance, correlation, or coupling kernel. [R-AE 13-14]
Why it matters: The word cross suggests interaction between two objects, but the definition merely multiplies their magnitudes.
Repair: Either rename it or define an inner-product or bilinear pairing that contains genuine cross terms.
5. The “duality” theorem is commutativity dressed as structure  [Critical]
Evidence: The proof unfolds the definition, applies double conjugation, and closes with mul_comm. [R-AE 27-34]
Why it matters: A theorem whose decisive step is x*y=y*x does not justify a new duality theory.
Repair: Rename it factor_swap and state plainly that it is an elementary invariance.
6. The paper markets tautologies as research contributions  [Major]
Evidence: The nonnegativity theorem is mul_nonneg for two norms. Both zero-locator theorems are zero_mul or mul_zero. [R-AE 15-19, 44-59]
Why it matters: Machine-checking does not transform elementary algebra into a new analytic result.
Repair: Present these as helper lemmas, not headline theorems.
7. The “Universal Zero Locator” locates nothing  [Critical]
Evidence: The theorem requires the exact zero location as a hypothesis and concludes only that a product containing that zero factor vanishes. [R-AE 44-51]
Why it matters: A locator should infer or constrain where a zero is. This statement consumes the location and discards information.
Repair: Rename it crossNormProduct_eq_zero_of_left. For a locator concept, prove a converse or a quantitative detection theorem under explicit nonvanishing assumptions on the other factor.
8. The word “universal” exploits a dummy parameter  [Major]
Evidence: Once the left factor is zero, sigma2 can be anything because the entire product is zero. The universal quantifier expresses no uniform analytic control. [P 57-66; R-AE 47-59]
Why it matters: The wording turns vacuous parameter independence into a dramatic global theorem.
Repair: State the pointwise lemma without promotional language.
9. No converse means the product cannot identify which factor vanished  [Critical]
Evidence: crossEnergy = 0 is compatible with either the left or right norm being zero. The paper never proves or discusses this ambiguity.
Why it matters: Even as a detector, the quantity cannot distinguish the alleged source of the zero.
Repair: Prove E=0 iff left=0 or right=0 using nonnegativity of norms, then explain that it still does not locate a zero without additional information.
10. “Sharp” is asserted without an optimality result  [Major]
Evidence: crossEnergy_ge_re uses abs_re_le_norm and norm_mul. No equality characterization or best-constant theorem is present. [R-AE 35-43]
Why it matters: Sharpness is a separate mathematical claim. It cannot be inferred from the existence of an inequality.
Repair: Delete “sharp” or prove the constant 1 is optimal and state equality conditions.
11. “Bilinear” is inaccurate  [Major]
Evidence: The expression uses conjugated coefficients and norms. As a function of the coefficient sequence, conjugation is antilinear and the norm product is nonlinear. [R-DD 15-17; R-AE 13-14]
Why it matters: The label obscures the algebraic type of the construction.
Repair: Use sesquilinear only for a correctly defined pairing, and do not call the norm product bilinear.
12. The inequality is described backwards  [Moderate]
Evidence: The displayed theorem is |Re(product)| ≤ crossEnergy. Calling it a “lower bound” is defensible only if one says crossEnergy is bounded below by the real-part magnitude. The current title invites confusion.
Why it matters: Readers can reasonably expect a lower bound on the bilinear product rather than an upper bound on its real part.
Repair: Rename to realPart_abs_le_crossNormProduct.
13. The Guth-Maynard connection is rhetorical, not mathematical  [Critical]
Evidence: The project contains no large-value set, no separated set of frequencies, no additive energy argument, no counting measure, no N(sigma,T), no asymptotic notation, and no exponent proof. [GM; R-DD; R-AE]
Why it matters: The manuscript borrows contemporary relevance without formalizing any part of the cited breakthrough.
Repair: Move Guth-Maynard to a short future-work paragraph or formalize a specific intermediate lemma from their paper.
14. “DirichletDensity” contains no density theory  [Major]
Evidence: The module defines a finite sum, a norm square, conjugation identities, a threshold equivalence, and zero conjugation. It defines no density function or counting problem. [R-DD]
Why it matters: The filename and comments suggest a level of analytic content that is absent.
Repair: Rename the module DirichletPolynomialConjugation.
15. The “zero-density mollifier” comment is fictitious  [Major]
Evidence: dirichletPoly_zero_dual is a conjugation lemma. There is no mollifier and no density argument in its statement or proof. [R-DD 69-80]
Why it matters: The comment is materially misleading.
Repair: Replace the comment with “zeros are preserved under coefficient and argument conjugation.”
16. The large-value theorem has no large-value analysis  [Moderate]
Evidence: largeValue_dual_iff merely rewrites equal norms across conjugation. It proves no estimate on how often a threshold is exceeded. [R-DD 61-68]
Why it matters: The name can be mistaken for a result in the Guth-Maynard sense.
Repair: Rename threshold_iff_conj and reserve “large-value estimate” for cardinality or measure bounds.
17. The threshold V is unconstrained  [Moderate]
Evidence: The theorem permits any real V, including negative values, for which both inequalities are automatically true because norms are nonnegative. [R-DD 62-64]
Why it matters: This is formally harmless but semantically sloppy for a “large-value” condition.
Repair: Add 0 ≤ V when presenting the result as a large-value statement, or state that the unrestricted form is an algebraic convenience.
18. The paper and Lean definition disagree about the index set  [Critical]
Evidence: The paper assumes S is contained in positive naturals. The Lean definition accepts Finset Nat and explicitly handles n=0 under complex powers. [P 26; R-DD 12-45]
Why it matters: This is not a cosmetic mismatch. Zero is not a standard Dirichlet-polynomial index, and cpow at zero introduces special conventions unrelated to the intended mathematics.
Repair: Use a positive-natural subtype, sum over n+1, or require 0 notin S.
19. The code spends proof effort defending an unintended zero index  [Major]
Evidence: The longest case analysis in dirichletPoly_conj exists because n=0 was allowed. [R-DD 30-45]
Why it matters: The implementation complexity is self-inflicted and weakens correspondence with the manuscript.
Repair: Repair the type first, then simplify the conjugation proof.
20. “Off-line height” misuses standard terminology  [Moderate]
Evidence: The code uses sigma for the real part and t for the imaginary coordinate. The paper repeatedly calls sigma a height. [P 6, 28, 40, 59; R-PW 10-16]
Why it matters: In zeta-zero terminology, height normally refers to the imaginary part t.
Repair: Call sigma a real part or horizontal coordinate and t the height.
21. “Off-line” is undefined and unrestricted  [Major]
Evidence: The declarations accept every real sigma and t. They do not define the critical strip, critical line exclusion, nontrivial zero, or off-line predicate. [R-PW; R-AE]
Why it matters: The terminology suggests a relationship to the Riemann Hypothesis that the types do not encode.
Repair: Define CriticalStrip, CriticalLine, NontrivialZero, and OffCriticalLineZero predicates, or remove the term.
22. The abstract and code disagree on the sigma domain  [Major]
Evidence: The abstract claims sigma1 and sigma2 are in (0,1), while the Lean definitions and theorems take arbitrary reals without hypotheses. [P 6; R-AE]
Why it matters: The manuscript’s formalization map is inaccurate.
Repair: Either add interval hypotheses where they matter or state that the formal results are global algebraic identities.
23. dualOffLinePoint is dead code  [Minor]
Evidence: The definition is introduced in PhaseWinding but the subsequent theorems use offLinePoint (1-sigma) (-t) directly. [R-PW 14-16]
Why it matters: Dead definitions make the architecture look more developed than it is.
Repair: Use the definition consistently or delete it.
24. PhaseWinding has neither phase nor winding  [Major]
Evidence: The module contains coordinate identities and a wrapper around the completed-zeta functional equation. No argument function, winding number, contour, homotopy, or topological degree appears. [R-PW]
Why it matters: The filename materially misdescribes the content.
Repair: Rename the module CompletedZetaReflection.
25. “Topological symmetry constraint” uses no topology  [Major]
Evidence: offLine_zero_fourfold_symmetry is a rewrite using an equivalence already proved. [R-PW 47-51]
Why it matters: The comment falsely suggests topological machinery.
Repair: Call it zero_reflection_of_functionalEquation.
26. The “fourfold” theorem proves only one partner  [Critical]
Evidence: From a zero at sigma+it, the declaration proves a zero at 1-sigma-it. It does not prove zeros at sigma-it and 1-sigma+it, nor package four points. [R-PW 48-51]
Why it matters: The theorem name and manuscript table claim more than the proposition.
Repair: Add a conjugation theorem for completed zeta and prove all four members, or rename the current theorem to reflected_zero.
27. The paper itself confuses twofold reflection with fourfold symmetry  [Major]
Evidence: Its scope section labels the equivalence Lambda(sigma+it)=0 iff Lambda(1-sigma-it)=0 as a “fourfold” constellation. [P 74-75]
Why it matters: An equivalence between two points is not a four-point orbit.
Repair: Write the actual orbit and prove each map.
28. The completed-zeta reflection is inherited, not a new zeta theorem  [Major]
Evidence: completedRiemannZeta_offLine_functional_eq is Mathlib’s completedRiemannZeta_one_sub after a coordinate rewrite. [R-PW 29-35; M-Zeta]
Why it matters: The paper does not distinguish imported mathematical content from new glue code.
Repair: Mark inherited results explicitly and measure the original contribution honestly.
29. Several zeta theorems are redundant wrappers  [Moderate]
Evidence: The zero equivalence, norm equality, and one-way zero implication all follow immediately from the same imported equality. [R-PW 36-51]
Why it matters: The theorem count inflates the apparent body of work.
Repair: Group these as corollaries and avoid using theorem count as a quality metric.
30. HardyZ does not define Hardy Z  [Critical]
Evidence: The file defines criticalLinePoint and equalities for completedRiemannZeta. No theta function or real-valued Hardy Z-function exists. [R-HZ]
Why it matters: The module title and theorem name are false descriptions.
Repair: Either implement the classical Hardy Z-function and prove its real-valuedness and zero correspondence, or rename the module CriticalLineCompletedZeta.
31. hardyZ_abs_eq_riemannZeta_abs mentions neither Hardy Z nor riemannZeta  [Critical]
Evidence: Its statement compares norms of completedRiemannZeta at t and -t. [R-HZ 49-53]
Why it matters: The declaration name is objectively inaccurate.
Repair: Rename it completedRiemannZeta_norm_criticalLine_neg.
32. completedRiemannZeta_criticalLine_neg is reflexive after argument equality  [Major]
Evidence: The theorem first proves criticalLinePoint(-t)=1-criticalLinePoint(t) and rewrites. It does not use the functional equation. [R-HZ 28-37]
Why it matters: The comment calls this invariance of the function when it is only equality of the two input expressions.
Repair: Replace it with an argument identity lemma and use that lemma in the actual functional-equation theorem.
33. The boundary nonvanishing theorem silently includes the pole  [Critical]
Evidence: riemannZeta_ne_zero_boundary quantifies over all real t. At t=0 the argument is s=1, where the classical zeta function is undefined. Mathlib assigns a totalized value, and its documentation explicitly calls it a junk value. [R-NV 14-18; M-NV; M-Zeta]
Why it matters: The paper reports a formal artifact of totalization as ordinary analytic nonvanishing.
Repair: Require t != 0, or state explicitly that the t=0 case refers only to Mathlib’s totalized function.
34. The manuscript ignores completed-zeta totalization at s=0 and s=1  [Major]
Evidence: Mathlib documents that completedRiemannZeta is mathematically undefined at 0 and 1 but receives assigned values in Lean. The project’s global statements do not mention this. [M-Zeta]
Why it matters: Without the caveat, readers may treat total-function equalities as statements of meromorphic functions at their poles.
Repair: Add hypotheses excluding 0 and 1 for analytic interpretations, or use the entire pole-removed function completedRiemannZeta0 with precise notation.
35. Nonvanishing.lean contributes no new proof  [Major]

Bottom line
The Lean files may be type-correct, but the paper is not an analytic number theory result. It is a small collection of elementary identities and direct wrappers around Mathlib, presented with terminology that repeatedly exaggerates novelty, depth, and consequence. The largest claims are not merely optimistic. Several are category errors.
The central failure
A product of two norms is called an operator. Commutativity of multiplication is called a duality. The fact that zero times anything is zero is called a universal zero locator. One reflected zero is called a fourfold constellation. A completed-zeta symmetry is placed in a HardyZ module without defining Hardy Z. A Mathlib theorem about a totalized value at the pole is reported as ordinary nonvanishing of the classical zeta function at every point on Re(s) = 1. These labels manufacture importance that the statements do not possess.

Replacement text
We formalize several elementary conjugation and norm identities for finite Dirichlet polynomials in Lean 4. The development defines finite Dirichlet-polynomial evaluation, conjugated coefficient sequences, norm-square energy, and a product of norms evaluated at two complex points. We prove conjugation invariance, threshold equivalence, and immediate vanishing consequences when one factor is zero. We also record coordinate-level corollaries of Mathlib’s completed Riemann zeta functional equation and nonvanishing theorem. These results do not establish a zero-density estimate, a zero-locating method, or a new theorem about the Riemann zeta function. The contribution is a small, machine-checkable collection of reusable elementary lemmas.
Replacement text
The project verifies a modest set of algebraic identities for finite Dirichlet polynomials and restates selected Mathlib results in coordinate form. Its current value is pedagogical and infrastructural. Future work would need formal definitions of large-value sets, quantitative estimates, and a proved connection between finite Dirichlet polynomials and zeta before any zero-density or localization claim could be justified.

Final judgment
The present manuscript should not be circulated as a research contribution in analytic number theory. The paper’s framing repeatedly upgrades elementary consequences into grand concepts that the formal statements do not support. The appropriate response is not cosmetic editing.
